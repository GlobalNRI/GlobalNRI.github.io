Netherlands Visa Types: Complete Guide

Work, study, family, business and visitor routes, with 2026 income thresholds, application costs and timelines.

Important: This guide is for general information, not immigration or legal advice. Amounts below are based on IND information available in 2026. Salary and income thresholds can change during the year; confirm the exact amount, eligibility and fee on the linked IND page before you apply. Rules depend on nationality, purpose of stay and personal circumstances.

Choose the right route

EU/EEA and Swiss citizens generally do not need a Dutch residence permit to live and work in the Netherlands. Most other nationals who plan to stay longer than 90 days need a residence permit. Some also need an MVV (authorisation for temporary stay, usually a long-stay entry visa). The MVV and residence permit are often requested together through the Entry and Residence Procedure (TEV).

Visit up to 90 days

For tourism, family visits or short business trips. Whether you need a Schengen visa depends on your nationality. A short-stay visa does not grant a right to take a job.

Work or relocate

Common options are Highly Skilled Migrant, EU Blue Card, regular paid employment (often GVVA), researcher, self-employed or start-up permits.

Study or join family

A recognised educational institution normally applies for a student permit. A spouse, registered partner or qualifying unmarried partner may apply to join a resident sponsor.

Student visa and study residence permit

For a non-EU/EEA student on a programme longer than 90 days, the Dutch university or other eligible educational institution must normally be an IND-recognised sponsor and submit the application. Admission alone does not grant permission to reside.

  • Admission: Be accepted to a full-time programme at an institution recognised by IND; meet its academic and any progress requirements.
  • Living funds: The 2026 IND study norm for higher professional education or university is €1,130.77 per month. This is proof of means for living costs, separate from tuition. The amount and accepted evidence can vary by education type and must be checked for the relevant application period.
  • Study progress: Students generally need to achieve at least 50% of the annual study credits, subject to exceptions and the institution's reporting duties.
  • Working while studying: A student may work as an employee for up to 16 hours per week during the academic year, or full-time in June, July and August. The employer needs a work permit (TWV). Work as a self-employed person is subject to different conditions.
  • After graduation: Eligible graduates may apply for the one-year orientation year. It allows work without a TWV and can help transition to a qualifying job route.

See the current IND study amounts and have the university confirm its application checklist and deadline.

Partner and family permits

A Dutch resident or citizen may be able to sponsor a spouse, registered partner, unmarried partner in a durable relationship, or child, depending on the sponsor's status and the family member's situation.

  • The sponsor and applicant generally need to meet age, identity, relationship and residence conditions. An unmarried couple may need evidence that the relationship is genuine and durable.
  • The sponsor generally needs sufficient, independent and sustainable income. For a partner application, IND's standard 1 July to 31 December 2026 amount is €2,337 gross SV salary per month excluding holiday allowance (€2,523.96 including it). Other family situations can use different amounts.
  • Some applicants must pass the civic integration examination abroad before an MVV application; exemptions apply in some cases.
  • Work rights depend on the sponsor's permit and the wording on the family member's residence document. Check the work endorsement before accepting employment.

See IND's pages for partner permits and current family income requirements.

Visitor and short-stay visas

A Schengen short-stay visa is for a visit of up to 90 days in any 180-day period across the Schengen area. Visa-exempt travellers are still subject to the 90/180-day limit. Common evidence includes a valid travel document, purpose and itinerary, accommodation, sufficient funds or a sponsor, travel medical insurance and evidence you will leave before the permitted stay ends. The application is normally submitted through the Dutch embassy/consulate or its designated visa centre in your country of residence.

A short-stay visitor visa does not convert into a work permit. For residence longer than 90 days, check the applicable MVV and residence permit process before travelling.

Typical application timeline

  1. Confirm the route: Check nationality-based visa requirements, qualifying purpose, sponsor recognition, salary or funds, and whether an MVV is required.
  2. Prepare evidence: Gather passport, signed contract or admission letter, education and relationship documents, proof of funds, and any translations, legalisations or civic integration result required.
  3. Submit and pay: The employer, university, family sponsor or applicant files as required. Keep the receipt and application reference.
  4. Wait for the decision: Use the IND statutory decision period as the planning baseline; incomplete applications or additional checks can extend the total process.
  5. Collect the MVV if applicable: After approval, make the required appointment at the Dutch representation abroad and travel within the MVV validity period.
  6. After arrival: Collect the residence card, register with the municipality (BRP) if staying over four months, obtain a BSN, arrange health insurance where required, and complete any TB test requirement stated by IND.

Common documents to prepare

  • Valid passport and copies of relevant pages; passport validity rules may differ by application.
  • Application form, photograph, fee payment and appointment confirmation.
  • Employment contract and salary details, university admission and study-funding evidence, or sponsor/relationship records, depending on route.
  • Birth, marriage or other civil-status certificates where required. Documents may need legalisation/apostille and a sworn translation.
  • For a visitor visa: travel medical insurance, itinerary, accommodation, financial evidence or sponsorship, and evidence of ties/return plans as applicable.

Use the route-specific IND checklist rather than treating this as a complete document list. Names, dates and spellings should match across passports and civil documents.

From temporary residence to permanent residence

Permanent residence is a separate application, not an automatic upgrade after receiving a work or study permit. Many applicants need five consecutive years of lawful residence, a qualifying current residence purpose, sufficient sustainable income, registration and civic integration at the required level. Some periods or residence purposes may not count in the same way, so check your personal residence history and the latest IND rules before relying on a five-year calculation.
